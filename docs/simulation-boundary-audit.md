## 3. Historical scores and feature flow

The unchanged [buildGamePrior](C:/Users/eturn/Desktop/nfl-anayltics-app/lib/live-game-model.mjs:48) receives only rows accepted by the simulation's regulation gate. Selected-game, same-day, future and older-than-450-day histories are excluded. The same regulation values feed **league mean, home advantage, offense, opponent allowed scoring and variance**. There is no final-score fallback for a missing regulation row.

Recency remains `2^(-ageDays/halfLife)`. League mean has 40 equivalent observations at the configured mean. Team estimates retain their existing shrinkage. Scoring centers remain half team offense plus half opponent allowed scoring, with the existing venue adjustment and bounds. NBA/WNBA use the weighted variance; NFL/MLB use their inherited event/run distributions. No weights or distribution constants were tuned.

| Sport | Accepted evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| NFL | `overtime=0`: final equals regulation. `overtime=1`: tied end-of-fourth record using raw quarter, clock, play ID and total scores; must not exceed finals. | Never infer regulation from the losing final. Missing flag/PBP excludes the row. The normal app reads available local PBP instead of downloading every historical season. |
| NBA/WNBA | Completed four-period final, or first four quarter scores from a complete OT summary. All periods must reconcile with both finals; OT regulation must be tied. | Supports numeric `value` and ESPN `displayValue`; summary period count can come from matching complete linescore lengths. ## 4. NFL overtime

The production simulation **does not draw overtime outcomes**. The deterministic kernel uses the supplied NFL season, not the calendar year of a January playoff. Unknown season means no rule profile.

| Season | Regular season kernel | Postseason kernel |
|---|---|---|
| 1999–2009 | Sudden death, 15 minutes | Sudden death, 15-minute periods |
| 2010–2011 | Sudden death, 15 minutes | Modified sudden death |
| 2012–2016 | Modified sudden death, 15 minutes | Modified sudden death |
| 2017–2021 | Modified sudden death, 10 minutes | Modified sudden death |
| 2022–2024 | Modified sudden death, 10 minutes | Both initial possession opportunities |
| 2025–2026 | Both opportunities, subject to 10-minute limit | Both opportunities, 15-minute periods |

Historical transitions: [NFL 2019 history](https://operations.nfl.com/media/3823/2019-nfl-record-and-fact-book.pdf), [2017 guide](https://operations.nfl.com/media/2700/nfl-2017-kickoff-guide.pdf), [2022 digest](https://operations.nfl.com/media/6101/2022-record-and-fact-book.pdf), [2025 changes](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/featured-rules). Current ordinary branches: [2026 Rule 16, printed page 65](https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf).

Tests cover opening FG/TD, the required reply, matching scores, supplied offensive tries, no-score possessions, defensive TD/safety, regular-season expiration and postseason possession continuation into period two. Unsupported events and unresolved transitions into period three throw. Kick recoveries, defensive try returns, penalties, replay, timeout/clock administration and later-half kickoff procedures remain outside this kernel. It assumes adjudicated ordinary alternating possessions.

Raw PBP has drive identifiers/results, possession teams and clocks. A validated joint generator for drive outcome, duration, field position, kick recoveries and try strategy is absent. The kernel does not invent those probabilities. Completing/fitting that generator on a declared development period and evaluating prospectively is required before restoring final-game probabilities.

## 5. MLB rules and limits

The cache inventory found one detailed MLB `feed/live` response with batting events and runner movements. The 2022–2025 training files contain player-game box-score aggregates, not full base/out/advancement transitions. They do not support a validated event-conditioned generator.

The supplied-play helper stops ordinary walk-offs at the winning run and counts the batter plus verified runners on an out-of-park homer. Tests cover a single, bases-loaded homer and skipped bottom ninth. Runs must already be adjudicated for force/appeal outs. [2026 MLB Rule 7.01(e), printed page 94](https://mktg.mlbstatic.com/mlb/official-information/2026-official-baseball-rules.pdf).

The actual sampler still draws geometric runs per out and caps aggregate bottom-nine-or-later walk-offs at a one-run win. It cannot reliably estimate exact margins, run-line chances or homer overshoots. Labels disclose this; **all MLB market comparisons are withheld**. The helper is not presented as an event generator.

Automatic-runner eligibility is MLB regular season, 2020–2026, never postseason. Only nine-inning schedules are supported; unknown formats and future unverified years are withheld. The inherited `.48` scoring chance remains approximate. [MLB runner history](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/rules/designated-runner).

Needed work: a broad chronological batting/base-out/advancement corpus, explicit batting exposure, and separately trained/tested event generation. No homer or runner-transition rates were guessed from inspected evaluation results.

## 6. Publication timing

The schedule receipt is dated September 23, 2026; relevant PBP copies were fetched/cached in 2026. Those times document later copies, not pregame publication in 2023–2025. The 2025 PBP metadata lacks a SHA-256 binding, so its byte hash is computed and `fetchedAt` reported as null rather than attaching an unbound timestamp.

Each forecast records actual prior IDs, cutoff, seed/count, fingerprint and source hashes. **Zero historical rows have immutable pregame publication proof.** Chronological filtering excludes selected/future game outcomes but cannot undo corrections. Current rosters, availability, weather and odds are not game-engine inputs; separate player cards are excluded from this evaluation.

API `publicationMode=faithful` and evaluator `--faithful` withhold results; the latter reports zero evaluated games. Only the explicitly labeled retrospective mode produces metrics. Fidelity requires immutable as-published snapshots and a row-level availability ledger tied to every cutoff.

## 10. How it now works

Choose a game in Simulation within the Models workspace, 1,000–50,000 runs and an optional seed. The app loads earlier completed games, separates regulation scores, excludes unverifiable rows, then builds the unchanged weighted offense/defense prior. Recency, shrinkage and scoring-distribution assumptions have the largest direct effects. Injuries, weather, lineups and shared game pace are absent.

One NFL scenario draws scoring events and stops after regulation. Basketball draws two rounded scores, adding approximate five-minute periods on a tie. MLB samples half-inning runs, skips unnecessary home batting, applies the disclosed aggregate walk-off cap and continues ties with the eligible runner approximation. Strength stays fixed across runs; noise represents scoring variation, not parameter uncertainty.

Repeated scenarios yield proportions, average scores, medians and middle-80% ranges. NFL probabilities mean **leading after regulation**; a tie means **OT required**, not a final NFL tie. Other sports retain approximate full-game estimates; MLB margins cannot reliably settle run lines. JSON consumers must read `outcomeScope`, `continuation` and `marketComparison` alongside retained probability field names. More runs reduce sampling noise, not model error.

Player props remain separate existing forecasts, neither draws from the game simulation nor constrained to its score. Seed/count changes do not change them. Expand limitations/inputs/validation or download JSON for excluded IDs and receipts. Run `npm start`; use `/nfl/simulation`, `/nba/simulation`, `/wnba/simulation` or `/mlb/simulation`. Older dates may exclude histories or withhold results unless their raw PBP/period evidence is available. Reproduction requires the frozen bytes identified above.
